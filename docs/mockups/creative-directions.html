Departures

The idea. Submitting a paper is a trip: you pick a destination, you have a departure time, and you need time to get a visa. So the Conference Planner becomes an airport departures board, and each submission plan becomes a boarding pass.

Why it works for first-timers. Everyone already knows how to read a departures board. Days left are large split-flap digits, and the status column does the warning work in words, not just colors.

Orbit

The idea. Your research question sits at the center, and your papers orbit it. The inner ring holds papers you've read, the middle ring the ones you're reading, and the outer ring your to-read list. Thin lines show which papers cite each other, and your thread is one bright path.

Why it works. Progress becomes visible: as you read, papers move inward. Try tapping the dots. On phones and for screen readers, the same data is available as a plain list.

Marginalia

The idea. Researchers scribble in the margins of printed papers. Here your review reads like a printed page: the research question is set huge, papers form a numbered index, and your notes appear in the margin beside each one, in italics, like handwriting.

Search as a card catalog. Finding papers works like a library drawer: you type into a sentence, and results come back as index cards, 20 to a drawer.

Which to build

Mix them by module. Departures for Ayush's Conference Planner and Marginalia for your Review Workspace give each half its own personality while sharing the palette and type.

Orbit as the signature view. It's the most memorable screen, so make it a "Map view" toggle on the review page once the list version works.

All three are plain HTML, CSS, and hand-drawn SVG, so they fit your no-framework rule. Titles, authors, and conference names are placeholders.
